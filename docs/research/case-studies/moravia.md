# Moravia as a learning case

Moravia is **not** Ourea’s current proving ground. It is a documented Medellín lesson about the limits of a purely technical response in an informal or historically marginalized neighborhood.

Ourea does **not**:
- treat Moravia as equivalent in landslide hazard to Llanaditas;
- invent community scores, acceptance probabilities or rankings for Moravia;
- alter the city-screen ranking to force Moravia into the shortlist;
- recommend resettlement as an automatic solution.

The current detailed sandbox remains Upper Comuna 8 / Llanaditas–El Faro. Moravia informs participation design and safeguards, not the optimizer.

## What the record shows

Moravia occupies a former municipal dump in Medellín’s Comuna 4. Displaced households, recyclers and low-income families settled the site and built livelihoods, social networks and territorial attachment around it. Later neighborhood-improvement and resettlement processes changed the physical landscape; they did not erase those social facts.

Vilar and Cartes (2016) document how urban design and social capital interacted in Moravia between 2004 and 2014. Physical upgrading and public facilities can strengthen a neighborhood, but they do not automatically preserve the relationships, work and everyday centralities that residents had already produced.

The [Sustainable Neighborhood Plan for Moravia](https://ecocitybuilders.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Moravia_Sustainable_Neighborhood_Plan_UNEP_English_10-01-18.pdf) treats the neighborhood as a living community with local economic activity, environmental recovery and resident participation, not as an empty slope waiting for an engineered intervention.

[Archivo Vivo de Moravia](https://archivovivomoravia.org/) and the [Living Archives / Archivo Vivo research](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/bartlett/research-projects/2024/jan/living-archives-archivo-vivo-weaving-gendered-histories-territorial-reclamation) record gendered histories of territorial reclamation. That archive is evidence that memory, care work and community organization are part of the neighborhood’s infrastructure.

Smith et al. (2020) reflect on a Medellín pilot of **negotiated** landslide-risk mitigation in informal settlements. A technically justified intervention can still fail socially when it ignores attachment to place, livelihoods, trust, local risk perception and the capacity to operate and maintain works after construction.

## Lessons that shape Ourea

Those sources do **not** supply numeric coefficients. They supply design constraints:

1. **In-situ adaptation first.** Compare physical adaptation portfolios on the hillside before presenting displacement as a default.
2. **Resettlement is out of prototype scope.** Ourea must not recommend relocation or score “social acceptability” of moving people.
3. **Technical robustness is not community validation.** A high-P10 portfolio can still be incomplete if livelihoods, accessibility, maintenance capacity or displacement risk have not been deliberated.
4. **Do not invent social data.** Absence of a community record means *not assessed*.
5. **Keep disagreement visible.** Do not average incompatible community positions into a false consensus score.
6. **Pilot, don’t pretend.** Legitimate representatives, data governance and veto criteria have to be agreed with residents. They are not already encoded in IMCV or stratum 1.

## How this appears in the product

- City equity still uses official IMCV/AMPI and stratum-1 exposure as **planning proxies**, with their documented limitations.
- Community Evidence & Safeguards records categorical consultation status, position, livelihood disruption, maintenance capacity, displacement risk and accessibility concern.
- High concerns mark a portfolio as **requires deliberation**.
- Participatory session inputs are labeled as such and exported in the decision package.
- The six-month pilot includes co-design activities; it does not invent confirmed partners or participation results.
